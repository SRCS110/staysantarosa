Winter WINEland 2027: Plan the Weekend from Santa Rosa

One ticket, one January weekend, dozens of northern Sonoma County wineries. Most of the planning happens when you buy.

  1. Home
  2. Blog
  3. Winter WINEland 2027: Plan It from Santa Rosa
← All stories

Stay Santa Rosa · Oct 28, 2026

Winter WINEland is the Wine Road's January open house: one ticket, one weekend, and dozens of northern Sonoma County wineries pouring for anyone wearing the event wristband. The 2027 edition runs Saturday and Sunday, January 16 and 17, 2027, from 11 a.m. to 4 p.m. both days. It sells out, there are no door sales, and most of the decisions that shape your weekend happen when you buy the ticket, so it pays to plan now.

Winter WINEland 2027 at a glance

  • Dates: January 16 and 17, 2027.
  • Pouring hours: 11 a.m. to 4 p.m. each day, whatever a winery's regular hours are.
  • Where: wineries across the Wine Road's three valleys, Alexander Valley, Dry Creek Valley and the Russian River Valley. The 2027 line-up has not been announced yet. The 2026 event had 63 wineries, in Healdsburg, Windsor, Santa Rosa, Geyserville, Sebastopol, Forestville, Guerneville and Cloverdale.
  • Tickets: online only and in advance. Wine Road says 2027 tickets go on sale later this year, and it has not published prices yet. A Sunday-only ticket is also offered.
  • Early access: Wine Road invites people to text WREVENTS to (707) 219-6623 for early ticket access. Wine Road Insider members get 20 percent off two tickets.

How the ticket works

A ticket covers tasting at every participating winery, with at least three tastes at each stop. The part first-timers miss is the starting winery. You pick it when you buy, and you cannot change it later. Each winery accepts a maximum of 50 check-ins, and full wineries are greyed out online, so the earlier you buy, the more choice you have about where your weekend begins.

At your starting winery you collect a tasting glass and a wristband, and you keep both for the whole weekend. Everyone in your party has to be there in person with photo ID. A lost wristband cannot be replaced without buying a new ticket, and a lost glass costs $5 to replace.

Sales are final and non-refundable, and tickets cannot be moved to other dates. They are transferable, though: the new guest needs a copy of the PDF ticket and valid ID, and checks in at the same starting winery. The event runs rain or shine.

Rules to know before you book

  • The event is strictly 21 and over. No children or babies.
  • Designated drivers must also be 21 or older. They get a different wristband color and no wine service.
  • Not every winery accepts groups of eight or more, and Wine Road asks guests not to rent a bus or travel in large groups.
  • Leave dogs at home.
  • Wineries will not serve anyone who appears intoxicated, even with a driver.

Why Santa Rosa works as a base

The three Wine Road valleys fan out from the north and west of town. The Russian River Valley begins on Santa Rosa's western edge, and most of its estates are twenty to twenty-five minutes from downtown. Dry Creek Valley and Alexander Valley, around Healdsburg, are about twenty-five minutes up Highway 101. That makes it easy to split the weekend by valley and come back to the same downtown each night instead of packing up a room.

A simple way to plan it:

  1. Saturday: start at a Russian River Valley winery near Santa Rosa, Sebastopol or Forestville, and work west.
  2. Saturday night: dinner downtown. Our downtown food guide has the options.
  3. Sunday: head north on Highway 101 to Dry Creek and Alexander Valley, around Healdsburg and Geyserville.

Your starting winery only applies to check-in, so choose one on the side of the county you want to cover first. For more on how the valleys differ, see our guide to wine tasting from Santa Rosa.

Planning for January

January is the middle of Sonoma County's wet season, so pack a rain layer and allow extra time on country roads. With a five-hour pouring window, it helps to be at your first winery close to 11 a.m. and to map a short loop rather than criss-crossing valleys. If the forecast turns, our rainy-day guide covers the indoor options back in town.

Good to know

  • Winter WINEland 2027: January 16 and 17, 11 a.m. to 4 p.m.
  • Tickets are online only, sell out and are non-refundable, but they can be transferred.
  • Your starting winery is fixed at purchase, and each winery takes up to 50 check-ins.
  • 21 and over only, including designated drivers.

Where to stay for Winter WINEland

A downtown base keeps the evenings simple after a day in the valleys. Hotel E is in a historic building on Old Courthouse Square, with continental breakfast before you set off, an evening happy hour when you get back and valet parking. Art House Santa Rosa is a few blocks away on 7th Street. Both put downtown dinner within walking distance, so nobody has to drive once the tasting is done.
