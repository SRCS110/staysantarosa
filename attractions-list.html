Arts & Culture
Santa Rosa Landmarks
Performing Arts
Luther Burbank Center for the Arts
50 Mark West Springs Rd · 5 min drive

The North Bay's premier performing arts center, Broadway tours, world-class music, comedy, dance, and speaker events in a 1,600-seat main hall. Over 50,000 children served annually through its education programs.

lutherburbankcenter.org →
Museum
Charles M. Schulz Museum
2301 Hardies Ln · 10 min drive

Dedicated to the life and work of Santa Rosa's most famous resident, the creator of Peanuts. Original comic strips, biographical exhibits, a recreation of Schulz's studio, and the beloved Snoopy's Home Ice rink next door.

schulzmuseum.org →
Historic Site
Luther Burbank Home & Gardens
204 Santa Rosa Ave · 5 min walk

The meticulously preserved home and experimental gardens of America's celebrated horticulturist, who developed over 800 new plant varieties. Free to visit and a genuinely peaceful downtown stop.

lutherburbank.org →
Historic District
Railroad Square Historic District
Historic 3rd St · 10 min walk

Built around the restored 1903 Northwestern Pacific Railroad depot, this charming district is lined with antique shops, independent restaurants, and coffee houses, and the starting point for the SMART commuter train.

railroadsquare.net →
Wildlife
Safari West
3115 Porter Creek Rd · 15 min drive

A 400-acre African wildlife preserve, dubbed the "Sonoma Serengeti", with giraffes, rhinos, zebras, cheetahs, and nearly 900 animals roaming free. Guided jeep tours and glamping tents available.

safariwest.com →
Museum
Museum of Sonoma County
425 7th St · 4 min walk

Rotating art exhibitions and historical displays exploring Sonoma County's cultural past and present, and conveniently right around the corner from the downtown hotels.

museumsc.org →
Outdoors
Nature & Coast
State Reserve
Armstrong Redwoods State Reserve
Guerneville · 35 min drive

Ancient coastal redwoods, some over 1,400 years old and 300 feet tall, tower above quiet forest trails near Guerneville. One of the most accessible old-growth redwood groves in Northern California.

parks.ca.gov →
Coastal
Bodega Bay
Sonoma Coast · 50 min drive

The dramatic Sonoma Coast, fresh Dungeness crab, rugged headland hikes, sheltered coves, and the village made famous by Hitchcock's The Birds. The drive alone through the coastal hills is worth the trip.

bodegabay.com →
State Park
Trione-Annadel State Park
Channel Dr, Santa Rosa · 15 min drive

Over 5,000 acres of forests, meadows, and streams with 40+ miles of hiking, biking, and equestrian trails right on Santa Rosa's eastern edge. Lake Ilsanjo is a popular fishing and picnic destination in the interior.

parks.ca.gov →
Regional Park
Spring Lake Regional Park
393 Violetti Rd · 15 min drive

Santa Rosa's 320-acre backyard, a swimming lagoon with lifeguards, boat rentals, hiking trails, and a hands-on natural history center. Popular on warm weekends with locals and visitors alike.

parks.sonomacounty.ca.gov →
River
Russian River
Guerneville · 30 min drive

In summer, the Russian River comes alive with canoeing, kayaking, swimming, and fishing. The Guerneville stretch is the most popular. Rent a canoe and float downstream through redwood-lined banks.

sonomacounty.com →
Adventure
Hot Air Ballooning
Various launch sites · Sonoma County

Drift above the vineyards at sunrise. Sonoma County's ballooning is among the most celebrated in California. Several outfitters launch from Santa Rosa, floating over the Russian River Valley and wine country hills.

sonomacounty.com →

Beyond the vineyards in Sonoma County

Wine is the reason most people come, and it is rarely the part they talk about afterwards. Santa Rosa is where Charles Schulz drew Peanuts for decades, and the museum built around his work is a short drive north of downtown. Luther Burbank spent fifty years breeding plants on a city block that is now a preserved home and garden. The Museum of Sonoma County holds both art and county history a few minutes' walk from Old Courthouse Square.

Outdoors, the county changes character quickly. Trione-Annadel State Park puts oak woodland and forty miles of trail in the hills at the edge of town. Spring Lake is the local lake loop. An hour west, Armstrong Redwoods holds old-growth coast redwoods, and beyond that the Sonoma Coast runs from Bodega Bay north along bluffs and coves. Safari West, north of the city, keeps a wildlife preserve you can tour and even sleep in.
